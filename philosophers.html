Welcome to the World of Philosophy!

Philosophy, from the Greek words "philo" (love) and "sophia" (wisdom), literally means "love of wisdom." The philosophers you'll meet today have shaped how we think about reality, ethics, politics, knowledge, and what it means to be human.

🤔 What is Philosophy?

The systematic study of fundamental questions about existence, knowledge, values, reason, mind, and language.

🏛️ Ancient Roots

Philosophy began in ancient Greece around 6th century BCE, with thinkers questioning traditional beliefs about the world.

🌍 Universal Impact

Philosophical ideas have influenced science, politics, religion, art, and every aspect of human civilization.

💭 Critical Thinking

Philosophy teaches us to question assumptions, analyze arguments, and think critically about complex issues.

Why Study These Great Thinkers?

The ten philosophers featured in this lesson represent some of the most influential minds in human history. Their ideas continue to shape our world today, from the democratic principles in our governments to the ethical frameworks we use to make moral decisions. By understanding their thoughts, we gain powerful tools for navigating our own lives and understanding our place in the universe.

Historical Timeline

Trace the development of philosophical thought through the ages:

c. 551-479 BCE
Confucius - Founded Confucianism in China, emphasizing moral conduct, respect, and social harmony
c. 470-399 BCE
Socrates - Developed the Socratic method of questioning; "The unexamined life is not worth living"
c. 428-348 BCE
Plato - Founded the Academy; developed Theory of Forms and ideal state concepts
384-322 BCE
Aristotle - Founded the Lyceum; systematic approach to logic, ethics, and politics
980-1037 CE
Ibn Sina (Avicenna) - Islamic Golden Age philosopher and physician
1596-1650
René Descartes - "Father of modern philosophy"; "I think, therefore I am"
1632-1704
John Locke - Empiricism and political liberalism; influenced American Constitution
1724-1804
Immanuel Kant - Critical philosophy; categorical imperative in ethics
1818-1883
Karl Marx - Historical materialism; critique of capitalism
1844-1900
Friedrich Nietzsche - "God is dead"; critique of traditional morality
