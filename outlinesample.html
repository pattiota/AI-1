Course Details

Course: BUS-101 – Introduction to Business

Class Time: 4 Hours

Topic: Types of Business Ownership

Course Overview

This comprehensive lesson explores the various forms of business ownership available to entrepreneurs and business owners. Students will learn to evaluate different ownership structures, understand their legal and tax implications, and make informed decisions about the most appropriate business structure for different scenarios.

Why This Matters

Choosing the right business structure is one of the most critical decisions an entrepreneur faces. It affects everything from personal liability and tax obligations to funding opportunities and growth potential. This lesson provides the foundational knowledge needed to make these important decisions.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

  1. Define and explain the concept of business ownership.
  2. Evaluate the advantages and disadvantages of each form of business ownership.
  3. Analyze the legal liability, tax implications, and control structures of various ownership types.
  4. Compare and contrast funding and investment opportunities by business type.
  5. Apply knowledge of business ownership to real-world scenarios and case studies.

🧠 Key Concepts

Core principles that guide business ownership decisions

  • Importance of business structure selection: The chosen structure affects all aspects of business operations, from daily management to long-term strategy.
  • Ownership and legal liability differences: Understanding how personal assets are protected or exposed based on business structure.
  • Tax treatment across structures: Different ownership types face varying tax obligations and opportunities.
  • Decision-making authority and control: How ownership structure affects who makes decisions and how they're made.
  • Funding and capital acquisition: The relationship between business structure and ability to raise capital.
  • Growth and exit strategies: How different structures support or limit business growth and owner exit options.

📝 Key Terms

Essential vocabulary for understanding business ownership

Sole Proprietorship

A business owned by one person with no legal distinction between owner and business.

Partnership

A business owned by two or more people who share profits, losses, and management.

Corporation

A legal entity separate from its owners, providing limited liability protection.

Limited Liability Company (LLC)

A hybrid structure combining corporation and partnership benefits.

Cooperative

A business owned and operated by its members for their mutual benefit.

Franchise

A business model where a franchisee operates using a franchisor's brand and system.

Limited Liability

Legal protection limiting personal financial responsibility for business debts.

Pass-through Taxation

Tax treatment where business income is taxed at the owner's personal rate.

Double Taxation

Taxation of corporation profits and again when distributed as dividends.

Articles of Incorporation

Legal documents filed to establish a corporation.

Franchise Agreement

Contract outlining the terms between franchisor and franchisee.

Business Structure

The legal and organizational framework of a business entity.

🎮 Activities & Assignments

Engaging exercises to reinforce learning

In-Class Activities

1. Business Ownership Comparison Table

Students fill out a matrix comparing all business types on:

  • Liability
  • Taxation
  • Decision-making
  • Capital
  • Example business

2. Ownership Role-Play Exercise

Groups simulate different ownership types making a business decision (e.g., expanding to a second location)

3. Kahoot or Quizizz Game

Quick interactive quiz to reinforce key concepts

4. Real Business Research

Assign each group a real business (e.g., Subway, REI, Apple) and have them identify and explain the ownership type

Homework Assignments

Written Reflection (1 page)

Prompt: "Which form of business ownership best suits your future goals and why?"

Research Assignment

Interview a small business owner and report on why they chose their ownership structure.
